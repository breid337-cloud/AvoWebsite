Getting Found in Basingstoke

Your website is done and it is a good one. It loads faster than almost any small business site we have measured, Google has it, and if somebody searches your name they find you first. But almost nobody searches your name. They search “dog walker basingstoke” — and for that, you do not appear at all.

Why that is

When someone looks for a dog walker, Google shows a little map with three businesses pinned on it, each with a star rating. Almost everybody taps one of those three. Below the map are the ordinary blue links, and most people never get that far.

To be in that map you need a Google Business Profile — a free listing, separate from your website. You do not have one. We searched Google Maps for your business name and for your phone number, and Google replied that it cannot find them.

So this is not a case of ranking low. Your business is simply not in the part of Google that people actually use to find a dog walker. Here is who is:

What would make the difference

In order. The first one matters more than everything else put together.

1
Do this one first

Put the business on Google

It is free, it takes about ten minutes, and it is the single thing standing between you and the map. One thing to check as you go: we found three reviews on your old site labelled as Google reviews, so it is possible a listing was started at some point and never finished. Google hides a listing that has not been verified, which would look exactly like this. If one turns up when you sign in, claim it rather than making a new one.

On your phone

  1. Open the Google Maps app and sign in with the Google account you want to own this — probably the one your business email uses.
  2. Tap your profile picture, top right.
  3. If you see Your Business Profile, tap it — a listing already exists and you just need to finish it. If you only see Add your business, tap that instead.
  4. Business name: Basingstoke K9 Companions — exactly that, matching your website and your Facebook page.
  5. Category: Dog walker.
  6. When it asks whether customers visit you at an address, say no, and set a service area instead — Basingstoke and the villages you cover. This is the important bit: see the note below.
  7. Add your number 07504 243208 and your website address.
  8. Google will ask to verify you, usually by video or a postcard in the post. Follow whatever it offers.

Say no to publishing your address. A dog walking business run from home is what Google calls a service-area business, and you are entitled to keep the address private. If you enter it as a shop address, your home goes on the map for anyone to see.

2
The thing that keeps working

Ask your customers for a Google review

Once the listing exists, reviews are what decide whether Google puts you on that map above or below the next dog walker. You already have lovely things written about you — the three on your website among them — but they only count for this if they are on Google.

Eight would put you on the map. You have almost certainly walked more than eight dogs whose owners would happily write a sentence if you asked.

Once you are verified

  1. In the Maps app, go to Your Business Profile.
  2. Find Ask for reviews and tap Copy link.
  3. Text that link to a customer when you drop their dog home — that is the moment they are most pleased with you.
  4. Send it to us as well, and we will put a button on the website.

Never offer anything in exchange for a review. Google removes them and can penalise the listing. Just asking is allowed, and it works.

3

Some directories have the wrong details

Your business is listed on sites you may never have signed up to — Yell, MapQuest, Cylex, the Basingstoke Gazette directory. They disagree with each other. One of them, Cylex, shows a landline starting 01256, and your correct number is 07504 243208. The postcodes differ between listings too.

Google reads all of these when it works out who you are and whether to trust you. A business that gives four different answers about itself looks less reliable than one that gives the same answer everywhere.

No rush, but worth an evening

  • Search your business name and see which directories come up.
  • Most have a “claim this listing” or “is this your business?” link. Claim it and correct the number.
  • The details to match everywhere: Basingstoke K9 Companions, 07504 243208, and your website address.
  • If a listing shows your home address and you would rather it did not, ask them to remove it.
4

The old Weebly site is still out there

Your previous site still exists and Google still has it. Anyone landing on it is sent straight to the new one, so nobody gets stranded — but it does mean two versions of your business are in Google competing with each other.

When you have a minute

If you still have the Weebly login, delete the site or unpublish it. Everything on it is on the new site already. If the login is long gone, leave it — the forwarding we put in place does the job and this is a small thing.
